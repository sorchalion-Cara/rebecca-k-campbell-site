Poetry

Other Writings

I began, in 2020, to set down short poems in free verse, a habit sparked by no single cause but by many: the turn of weather, the passing of a season, an undertaking begun or ended, the private ceremony of celebrating those I love best. Gathered here is a small selection of that work, arranged under two headings: African Bush and Further Afield. I hope, in reading them, that you find something of what moved me to set them down.

Category

African Bush

There is a bushcamp in the Timbavati, hard against the south-western boundary of the Kruger National Park, whose name translates simply as “place of rest.” For me it has been a good deal more than that: a place of healing in the wake of bereavement, a place of love, and, not least, of inspiration. What follows are four of the poems I composed there, offered as a small tribute to a place that gave back rather more than rest alone.

Further Afield

And further afield: verses written wherever wind, grief, or a mountain trail happened to carry me, up a hiking path at dusk, aboard a small boat given over to the sea, and into a room where old age keeps its long, quiet watch.

Sunset Hike

21 February 2023

The strong wind exhilarates. The sea below glitters in the setting Sun. Around us brilliant green is the mountainside. My guide, my dear friend, faces the still brilliant light closes her eyes straightens herself and stretches out her arms as if absorbing its rays to recharge her own natural radiance which blesses all who know her. I stand entranced joyful in this place this time and my companion. I am learning so much from her. She relaxes smiles and leads us on. Between us words are unnecessary for I would follow her anywhere.

The Schooner

22 September 2023

Ssssssh! seethes the sea as our bow slices the Atlantic rollers. Spray flies outward and upward as our small schooner clears the breakwater and confronts the ocean. How I have missed this! Sea and sky, wind and wave combine to exhilarate me! A joy, long forgotten, re-awakes and reinvigorates me. Excited, I forget to be sick. Wind, water, earth and fire! Fire burns in me and in my sisters. Already, I have connected with earth. Now, water calls to me, irresistibly. Surrounded by belovéd mountains, and by the even more loved sea, my life can finally begin.

(Note: aboard the Schooner Esperance, 7 September 2023, with my true family, to scatter my greatly beloved Zélia's ashes at sea, off Cape Town.)

Oncoming Night

31 January 2026

“Getting old” my Dad would say “is for the birds”. His voice was still strong then. No longer is this so. Alive but barely shrivelled and shrunken he seems like a corpse a ghost trapped in corporeality, hanging on for Mum's sake. Seventy years have they been married and it is difficult for him to say goodbye and impossible for her. She is still spritely in body at 95 but her mind alas is not. Seeing them to be frank is a strain. They are shadows of what they once were. Nightfall can be cold and cruel.
